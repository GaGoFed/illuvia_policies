## What the privacy policy says

Illuvia collects nothing. There is no account, no sync, no telemetry and no network access of
any kind — the package declares no network capability, so the app cannot open a socket even
if it wanted to. What you type stays in an encrypted database on your own PC (AES-256, with
the key held by Windows for your account), alongside a local diagnostic log.

Data leaves the machine only when you export a backup yourself, to a folder you pick. A backup
can be sealed with a password, and if it is not, it is readable by anyone who opens the file —
including any service passwords you saved on a payment plan, which is why the app warns you
before writing one.

Uninstalling does not delete your data: Windows removes the application and leaves the folders
in place, so reinstalling finds everything where you left it. The policy names the two commands
that do delete it, and the folder you can remove yourself.

## What the terms of use say

Illuvia is free, provided as is, and does the arithmetic on the numbers you enter — it is not
financial advice, and it does not talk to your bank. You are responsible for your own backups:
without one, a forgotten PIN or a lost encryption key means the data cannot be recovered, by
anyone. The app is for personal use; the source is proprietary, and the bundled Inter typeface
is used under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

Both documents are written to match what the app actually does. **If the app changes, they
change with it** — that has already been necessary twice, and a policy that describes an older
version of the app is worse than none.
